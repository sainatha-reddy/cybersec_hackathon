# Evaluation

Generated by `python benchmark/run.py --cases 300 --seed 2026` (300 randomised captures, 392 s).

Each case is a fresh 120-second synthetic capture: office background traffic at a random rate, 0–2 problems with randomised parameters (loss rate, scan speed and size, poisoning interval, outage length, number of forged answers, flood size, ...) placed in separate time slots, and 0–3 *hard negatives*: benign situations that resemble problems. A problem counts as **correct** when it is found with the right verdict, or is correctly linked as a consequence of another incident.

An `arp_conflict` next to `router_failover` is expected: in that hard negative a standby router announces the gateway address while the original keeps working, which is a genuine address conflict. It is reported as a FAULT, never as an attack.

## What the benchmark caught during development

The first 60-case trial scored 94% correct verdicts and exposed three weaknesses, which were then fixed:

* **Slow port scans** (about 12 probes/s) landed just below the attack threshold and were marked UNCERTAIN. Fix: the 'ports tried' clue now scales with the number of ports, and a new clue counts probes that hit closed ports or missing hosts.
* **Short DNS outages** were diluted by healthy traffic in the rest of the capture (failure ratio 23% overall). Fix: the failure ratio is measured over the worst 30-second window.
* **Busy clients** (many servers, all carrying data) produced a needless 'many connection attempts' incident. Fix: such sources are no longer reported unless they use stealth probe types.

## Honest caveats

* The captures are synthetic and written with the same packet primitives as the bundled samples, so this measures robustness to parameter changes, timing, background noise and combinations, not every quirk of real networks.
* Real captures were checked separately (public Wireshark sample captures: ARP storm, teardrop, DHCP, DNS, TCP ECN, SMB, an ISP router boot). Those tests found and fixed: clock jumps, DNS records without data, false alarms from client-side resets and a misleading label. They are not redistributed here because their licences are unclear.
